# Planning engine spike

Can a code-driven planning engine turn a lot and a zoning envelope into built-out plans (core, corridor, unit
layouts, parking, takeoffs) and emit an IFC model an architect can continue from, while still feeding the
existing Pascal massing view? This directory is the spike that answers it. It is a Python package with no
ties to the app; the roadmap's skills 6, 7 and 8 would wrap it as a tool.

```
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python example.py
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -p 'test_*.py'
```

Site assembly passes `parcels[].polygons`, each with an exterior `polygon` and interior `holes`.
The JavaScript assembly module and `assemble.py` use this protocol and must be updated together.
The returned convenience outline is available for one hole-free part. Complex assemblies retain
their parts and holes; the current single-ring envelope and massing tools require that limitation
to be resolved before using them for such a site.

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `layout.py` | A scheme's typical floor for the option cards: many layouts in the storey outline, checked against cited New York rules (`RULES`), scored, the best returned as an architecture plan with a "why this plan" report. See "Typical floors" below. |
| `engine.py` | Lot, storeys, unit mix and rules in; per-storey plans and takeoffs out. Feet, in the `acres_model_concept` frame (x along the frontage, z into the lot, street at z = 0). |
| `assemble.py` | Unions selected parcel polygons in feet, preserving interior holes and reporting net areas and contiguity. |
| `exporters/pascal.py` | Writes the exact `acres_model_concept` arguments, so the same building renders in the existing viewer; `scene` writes the detailed Pascal scene of an architecture plan. |
| `exporters/ifc.py` | Writes IFC4 with ifcopenshell: `IfcSite` > `IfcBuilding` > `IfcBuildingStorey`, an `IfcSlab` per storey, an `IfcSpace` per unit and common room, `IfcWall` along the corridor and around the core, in metres; `write_plan` writes the detailed model of an architecture plan. |
| `architecture.py`, `exporters/svg.py` | The detailed architecture plan v1 (walls, openings, rooms in metres), its validator and its dimensioned SVG. See `docs/architecture-plan.md`. |
| `exporters/package.py` | The architect's package, derived once from a plan for every file below: storeys (each level stands for the storeys it lists), units, door swings, door and window marks, area, unit, door and window schedules, the pre-filing self-check and the zip. |
| `exporters/ifc.py` `write_plan`, `exporters/arosa.ids` | The IFC4 model: a named storey at its elevation for every storey, walls, doors, windows, slabs with openings at stairs and elevators, a roof, stairs (7 in risers, 11 in treads), one elevator per stack, an `IfcZone` per unit, and property sets with the zoning and area figures (`Arosa_Zoning`, `Arosa_StoreyArea`, `Arosa_Unit`). It must pass schema validation and the IDS in `arosa.ids` (ifctester) before it is written. |
| `exporters/drawings.py` | The drawing set as one 17 by 11 in PDF: cover with the zoning table and assumptions, self-check, zoning diagram, site plan, a dimensioned plan per plate, elevations, a section through the core and schedules; plus `schedules.xlsx` and `selfcheck.json`. The survey caveat is in every title block. |
| `exporters/dxf.py` | The site plan and plates as a DXF (R2018, inches, AIA layers), each a block at true lot coordinates; it must pass ezdxf's audit. |
| `fixtures/` | The synthetic 10 by 8 m two-bedroom sample (`sample-floorplan.json` and its drawing) the cross-format gate runs on. |
| `example.py` | A 200 by 330 ft Lower Manhattan lot under a C5-5-style envelope: six-storey full-lot base (retail, office), 15-storey residential tower of 80 by 230 ft, one below-grade parking level. |
| `out/` | Generated by `example.py` (`concept.pascal.json`, `concept.ifc`, `takeoffs.json`); not tracked. |

## Typical floors (layout.py)

`run.py` takes `{"typicalFloor": {...}}` from `acres-research/architecture-tools.mjs` `schemeTypicalFloor`. The region
is the typical residential storey of the option's outlines (`outlines.py`), or a frontage-by-depth rectangle less a
rear yard. Candidates are wings along the street faces: a double-loaded corridor with stairs at its ends or beside
the elevator, a central core (stairs at the ends of a middle zone, or a cluster with a scissor stair), an L on a
corner, and a U or L along the side lot lines around a court. Units come from templates (studio, one bedroom, a
split two bedroom, two and three bedrooms off a hall, corner variants with a bedroom on the side wall), with the
kitchen open at the back of the living room. Each floor picks the scheme's unit types by largest share deficit, its
group (affordable or not) first, and deals the longest run of those picks its bays hold whole, a type that does not
fit drawn as a smaller one of its group, so a floor short of room keeps the affordable share. A unit is drawn only
whole: its template fits, its living room, bedrooms and halls stand inside the plate at their least sizes, and its
bath is whole; a space that holds no unit is drawn as a space too small for a unit, floor area outside any unit,
and the report counts those spaces over the building. Light and air, room sizes, egress, elevator, refuse and floor area are
measured on the drawn rooms, each check cited in `RULES`; values no section fixes are marked as assumptions. The
best score (net-to-gross, units against the scheme's count, window exposure, corner units, core efficiency, floor
area against the budget, less penalties for failed checks) ranks the plates; the building kept holds the most
zoning floor area as its sheet will account it, then the most units. The ground floor and cellar are a schedule in the
report (lobby, retail on a commercial overlay, refuse storage, bicycle room, recreation space, mechanical rooms),
not drawn levels.

## How the layout works

Every storey gets a corridor along its long axis, one stair at each corridor end, an elevator core at
mid-plate on one side of the corridor, and units filling the two bands between corridor and facade.
Unit widths come from the type's target area over the band depth; types are picked by largest share
deficit until a band segment is full, then stretched to fill it exactly, never below the minimum width.
The topmost residential storey sets the stair and core positions and every storey whose outline contains
them reuses them, so the core stacks through the base and the cellar. Non-residential storeys are open
plan around the stacked core, with a lobby from the street to the first stair at grade; a parking
storey gets a ramp and a stall count by area. Polygons are rectangles clipped to the storey outline with
shapely, so a simple non-rectangular outline still yields honest areas.

Rules carry their citation in `Rules` and in each check:

| Rule | Value used | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exits per storey | 2 (one exit allowed only for R-2 up to 4 storeys, 3 units per storey, 2,500 sq ft per storey, Type I or II, sprinklered) | NYC BC 2022 1006.3.1, 1006.3.2 |
| Exit separation | at least one third of the storey diagonal, sprinklered (one half unsprinklered; scissor stairs count as one exit) | NYC BC 2022 1007.1.1 exception 2 |
| Exit access travel distance | R and M 200 ft, B 300 ft, S-2 250 ft, sprinklered. NYC amends the IBC's 250 ft for R down to 200 ft. | NYC BC 2022 Table 1017.2 |
| Common path of egress travel | 125 ft, R-2 sprinklered | NYC BC 2022 Table 1006.2.1 |
| Dead-end corridor | 40 ft in R-2 (80 ft with a 2-hour enclosed corridor); 20 ft general, 50 ft sprinklered B, M, R-1 | NYC BC 2022 1020.4 exception 4 |
| Corridor width | 44 in minimum (36 in at 50 occupants or fewer, 30 in inside an R-2 unit); the engine uses 5 ft 6 in | NYC BC 2022 Table 1020.2 |
| Room sizes | one living room of 150 sq ft, others 80 sq ft with an 8 ft least dimension; the engine's 14 ft minimum unit width is a practical floor above that | NYC HMC 27-2074 |
| Parking space | 300 sq ft per space including aisles, stall at least 8 ft 6 in by 18 ft | ZR 25-62 |
| Manhattan Core parking | none required; as-of-right accessory spaces at most 20 percent of new units (CD 1 to 6) or 200 | ZR 13-10, ZR 13-11 |
| C5-5 bulk | FAR 15; C5 is the R10 equivalent; tower at most 40 percent of the lot, 15 ft from a wide and 20 ft from a narrow street when within coverage | ZR 33-122, ZR 34-112, ZR 33-451 |
| Dwelling unit factor | 680 | ZR 23-52 |

## Findings

**What worked.** The whole chain runs in about a second: 22 storeys laid out, 126 egress and depth
checks all passing, takeoffs, Pascal JSON and IFC written. The example produces:

| Takeoff | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Storeys above grade, height | 21, 235 ft |
| Gross floor area, FAR | 672,000 sq ft, 10.18 of 15 |
| Residential gross, net rentable, efficiency | 276,000 sq ft, 234,330 sq ft, 84.9 percent |
| Units | 285 (19 per typical floor: 4 studio, 8 one-bed, 5 two-bed, 2 three-bed), 968 gross sq ft per unit |
| Unit sizes achieved | studio 561, one-bed 710, two-bed 1,008, three-bed 1,329 sq ft (targets 450, 650, 950, 1,250) |
| Dwelling unit cap | 405 (680 factor on gross residential area) |
| Typical floor egress | travel 151 ft of 200, common path 54 ft of 125, dead end 0 ft of 40, stairs 218 ft apart against 81 ft required |
| Parking | 211 stalls achievable on the cellar, 57 as-of-right in the Manhattan Core |

The Pascal export was fed through the real `acres_model_concept` handler (`createConceptTools` with
`context: false`): it accepted the 21 levels, reported the same 672,000 sq ft and FAR 10.18, and the
Pascal scene validated. The below-grade storey is left out because the tool builds from the ground up.

**IFC verification.** Re-reading `out/concept.ifc` with ifcopenshell 0.9.0: schema IFC4, 1,725
entities, `IfcSite` 1, `IfcBuilding` 1, `IfcBuildingStorey` 22, `IfcSpace` 384, `IfcSlab` 22, `IfcWall`
118. `ifcopenshell.validate` reports no schema errors. Units are metres; a sampled one-bed space sits in
storey "Residential" at elevation 30.023 m with an absolute placement of 30.023 m and a 2.95 m
extrusion from a 0.25 m slab, and `ifcopenshell.geom` tessellates it (8 vertices, 12 faces). Each
space carries its area in `Description`, its unit type or room kind in `ObjectType`, and `PredefinedType`
`INTERNAL` or `PARKING`. Identical footprints share one extruded shape and the products in a storey
share one storey-relative placement, which is what keeps 22 storeys at 107 KB. The file has only been
checked with ifcopenshell, not opened in a desktop BIM tool; any IFC4 viewer should read it, and the first
real test should be Bonsai (BlenderBIM) or Solibri.

**Install.** The ifcopenshell 0.9.0 wheel installed cleanly on this Mac (Python 3.14, arm64); no STEP
fallback was needed. Validation with Express rules additionally needs `pytest`.

## Limits

- Rectangles only: every storey is a bounding-box plan. Courtyard, L and point-tower plans, setbacks
  within a storey and lot shapes that are not close to rectangular need their own layout strategies.
- Units are rectangles without rooms, kitchens, baths or windows; facade exposure and daylight are not
  checked beyond the 40 ft depth rule.
- The core is a single block; shafts, trash, mechanical rooms, mechanical floors and the roof bulkhead
  are not modelled, so efficiency is optimistic by a few points. Stairs are spaces without enclosure
  walls; corridor walls are centred on the corridor edge and overlap the units by 3 in.
- Travel distances are Manhattan paths from the farthest unit corner to the unit door and along the
  corridor to the nearer stair; dead ends are measured along the corridor past the last stair. These
  are planning checks, not a code review.
- The dwelling unit cap uses gross area as a stand-in for zoning floor area, and nothing checks yards,
  sky exposure, lot coverage or the envelope itself: the storeys are taken as given, which is the
  roadmap's skill 2 output.
- Base storeys are open plan around the stacked core; office net area ignores corridors and loading.
  Parking is a count by area with a ramp, not drawn stalls or aisles.
- The test-fit IFC (`write`) has no property sets, openings or doors; the detailed model (`write_plan`) has
  them. Neither is georeferenced (`IfcMapConversion`): the lot sits in the plan frame, not State Plane.

## Recommendation

Keep the planning kernel as code in Python, this engine or its successor, behind an `acres_plan_concept`
tool: shapely for the plan geometry, ifcopenshell for the handoff, and the existing concept JSON for the
Pascal view. The engine already produces every number skills 6, 7 and 8 need and the whole run is fast
enough to re-derive on each change, which is the roadmap's connecting principle. The alternatives looked
at do not replace it:

- **Hypar Elements** is a C# and .NET library, MIT licensed, "the smallest useful BIM", with JSON, glTF,
  DXF, SVG and IFC serialisation and a `Grid2d` for subdividing plates. It has no unit or corridor
  layout logic, so the planning code would still have to be written, in C# on a new runtime, and its IFC
  package is a separate project (2.3.0 stable, February 2025; 2.4.0 still alpha in May 2026). It is the
  right choice if the team wanted a typed element model with glTF output on .NET; for Arosa it adds a
  runtime without removing work.
- **Ladybug Tools dragonfly** (Python, AGPL-3.0) builds `Room2D` objects from floor-plate polygons and
  a floor-to-ceiling height, groups them into `Story` and `Building` with multipliers, and translates to
  Honeybee energy and daylight models. It can tell a core room from a perimeter room but generates no
  corridors, units or IFC. It belongs in a later energy and daylight step, with the AGPL licence
  considered before it is linked into a hosted product.
- **Blender bpy** is Blender as a pip module (5.2.2, September 2026, Python 3.13 only, a 245 MB
  macOS wheel, GPL-3.0), and `blender -b` renders without a display. Combined with the Bonsai add-on it
  reads and writes IFC through the same ifcopenshell. It is a renderer and editor, not a planning
  engine; Pascal already renders the massing and plans, so Blender would only be worth adding for
  photographic stills of the concept, as a separate service.

Next steps, in order: take the storey outlines and lot from skill 2's envelope instead of hand input;
add a courtyard strategy for deep plates and a point-tower strategy for small ones; model the stair
enclosures, shafts and a mechanical floor so efficiency is realistic; a second pass that lays out rooms
inside each unit; SVG plan export for skill 7; property and quantity sets in the IFC and a first open in
Bonsai; tests on real PLUTO lot shapes.
