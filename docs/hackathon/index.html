One more home in Astoria

At 23-29 Broadway, we explore whether another home could fit while retaining the existing building. The demonstration connects property and zoning sources to a preliminary comparison, an explicitly assumed second-floor plan and a walkthrough tied to a saved revision.

The assumed plan is twenty by eighty feet, with rooms, walls and openings that remain editable. Matching IFC, Pascal, Blender and browser artifacts help a builder or architect inspect the same design. Furniture, finishes and lighting are illustrative.

Modeling can begin without a financial model. Independent Underwriting explores costs, returns and land value when the next question is whether the idea could work economically.

Explore a New York housing idea

  1. Open Modeling, choose Sign in with Arosa and use your Google account. Verified free accounts have 25 research runs per month under the judging configuration.
  2. Enter a New York address, select the matching property and describe a housing goal. For the demonstrated example, use 23-29 Broadway, Astoria and ask about one more home while retaining the existing building.
  3. Inspect cited records, zoning sources and unresolved assumptions. Compare building options, or create an explicitly assumed schematic plan to explore design ideas.
  4. Save a plan revision, render its walkthrough and open the matching files. Use Underwriting separately to explore the numbers.

Property records and zoning research do not replace a survey, permit, professional design or approval. IFC import reports supported geometry and losses. Scan checks report alignment, face distances, tolerances and sampling. A conceptual image does not establish feasibility or exact placement.
