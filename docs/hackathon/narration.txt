Hello, my name is Michael Ort. I live in New York City. My mother grew up here in the seventies and eighties. I want the next generation to have room to build a life here, too.

New York completed about seventeen thousand homes a year in the seventies, and ten thousand in the eighties. Over the last decade, that rose to nearly twenty-eight thousand. We can build more. We have.

Yet more than half of households renting in New York spend at least thirty percent of their income on rent and utilities. Nearly three in ten spend half. Behind those numbers are families deciding what they can afford to give up.

I discovered an opportunity many people never get to explore: what could the land we already have become? With Muse, I built Arosa to help people ask that question, and see the possibilities for themselves.

Start with an address. Muse Spark researches the site through our tools, bringing sources and assumptions into view. In Astoria, we asked: could one more home fit while keeping the existing building?

Muse helps turn that question into a plan you can inspect. Rooms, walls, openings, and dimensions stay editable. Compare possibilities, revisit an assumption, and understand what still needs an architect's review.

A beautiful idea also needs workable numbers. With Muse guiding the conversation, Arosa keeps design and underwriting separate, so people can explore both a place to live and the economics of making it real.

Muse Wonder Full helped improve lighting and materials in our Blender test while preserving the original geometry. Muse Image works from a selected reference photograph to create a concept illustration, separate from measured plans.

These are early possibilities, not approved buildings. But clearer possibilities can start better conversations among homeowners, architects, and communities. Muse makes that first step more accessible, from a question to something people can examine together.

New York has always made room for ambition. With Muse, I want to help it make room for people. One lot. One possibility. One more home.
