Help/The three options

What are the three ways to build my site?

Each site gets up to three buildings, priced on their own, so you can see what zoning allows as of right against what a bonus or tax program adds.

The three options

As of right
The best building on standard floor area alone, with no affordability bonus and no tax program.
Most floor area
The largest building that uses the affordability bonus from City of Yes, 485-x or city financing, and of two that build as much, the one that pays more for the land. The bonus adds floor area, and the added area is rented at income-restricted rents (the HPD 60% AMI rent).
Alternative
The best remaining building that differs from the first two in exit (rental or condo) or program.

“Best” means the building that could pay the most for the land at your good-deal return, after construction, soft cost and carry. Land value explains the arithmetic.

In mandatory inclusionary housing areas the bonus is not available. Every new building sets aside a share of its floor area under a mapped option, and Arosa prices those options instead.

Each option states its affordable share, for example “44% affordable rentals at 60% AMI on average.” A condo’s affordable floor area is shown as rentals, because new affordable homes are almost all rented. The option that pays the most for the land says why in one sentence. Usually it is a building without the affordability bonus: every square foot the bonus adds must be affordable at 60% AMI on average, and that rent earns less than the floor area costs to build. Builders choose the same way. Of 139 new 485-x rental buildings registered outside mandatory inclusionary housing areas since City of Yes took effect in December 2024, about 95% took no bonus. Where the bonus pays for itself, or where city financing covers an all-affordable building, the winning option says so.

485-x, including the 99-unit option

485-x is the property tax exemption for new rental buildings of six or more units (RPTL 485-x). Arosa prices it as a choice:

  • 100 units or more: Option A, 25% of units at 80% AMI, with the construction wage floor. The wage floor adds to hard cost and shows as its own budget line.
  • Held to 99 units: Option B, 20% of units at 80% AMI, with no wage floor.

When a lot could hold 100 units or more, Arosa prices both. The 99-unit building competes only when it pays something for the land. 485-x applies to buildings that start construction by June 15, 2034.

Inputs and how fresh they are

Buildable area comes from zoning. Rents and sale prices come from listings and recorded closings, affordable rents from HPD, and 485-x rules from the statute. All sources and refresh times.

What it does not cover

  • Tax programs other than 485-x, and affordability programs other than the City of Yes bonus and mandatory inclusionary housing.
  • The 485-x construction period exemption, and which 485-x zone the lot sits in.
  • Condominiums with a tax program. 485-x applies to rentals only.

A lot with no bonus building shows no Most floor area option and says why.

How your own costs change it

All three options are priced again at your hard cost, soft cost, carry and target returns. Higher construction costs can push the winner from the 100-unit building to the 99-unit one, or from a bonus building to as of right.

Common questions

Why does the Alternative say it is the next best?

Every other building Arosa priced shares an exit and program with the first two, so none is a different kind of building. It shows the next best one and says so.
