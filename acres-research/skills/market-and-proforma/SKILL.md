# Market inputs, unit mix, pro forma and program choice

Market figures come from the user, from `acres_market_comps` (Realtor.com / MLS comparable harvesting via HomeHarvest), or from public sources you retrieved; no tool fabricates a missing rent or price. When the user has not supplied submarket prices or rents, call `acres_market_comps` (via `acres_execute_capability`) with the site's address, ZIP code, or `City, ST` to compute bedroom medians and days-on-market velocity. Supply a ZIP fallback explicitly only when it belongs to this requested location; another selected property's ZIP is not a default. Matching provider query results can populate `acres_market_inputs` and have listing citations when URLs are available. You can also record or override market figures directly with `acres_market_inputs`: residential rents and condo sale prices by bedroom count, commercial rents by use, existing supply with vacancy, the pipeline and monthly absorption, each with its source and retrieval date. Record only sections with evidence and state any conversion basis.

Read `provenance` before using comp results. Broader provider query targets and stored ZIP aggregates are reference-only: report their actual query locations, cache update time and scope limits, and keep the saved market inputs and unit mix unchanged. A cache update is not a listing retrieval date; a stored ZIP rollup is not filtered to the requested radius, time window or listing types. Requery a deliberately selected location for matching results, or record clearly labeled assumptions through `acres_market_inputs` when warranted by evidence. Cite `sourceId` only when one exists; do not invent a Realtor homepage source for a cache aggregate.

`acres_unit_mix` turns residential gross area into whole unit counts by bedroom type. Use nonempty `recommendedCondoMixByVelocity` or `recommendedRentalMixByVelocity` from matching comp results as `mixPercent` when appropriate. Reference-only results return no recommended mix. When mix, sizes or efficiency are planning assumptions, say so and prefer submarket evidence when available.

`acres_pro_forma` runs the development budget, revenue, NOI, yield on cost, value, returns and one-way sensitivities as code from the program, the recorded market inputs and the user's land price (or the ledger estimate below), costs, financing and operating assumptions. Record the market figures first. Report its assumptions and sources with the results, show the derivation the tool returns, and keep the judgment ("is this a good deal, what breaks it") in your answer rather than in the arithmetic.

## Commercial and mixed-use programs

Three product types: `rental` and `condo` for buildings with homes, and `commercial` for a wholly non-residential building (retail, office or community facility) held for its commercial income. Choose from the evidence, never by default: the parcel record's land use, building class and commercial area, the zoning's commercial and community-facility FAR (`commFar`, `facilFar` and the ceilings in the parcel diagnostics), any commercial overlay, and what the user is actually trying to do. When those point at commercial or mixed use, price that program; do not force a school, store, office or mixed-use building through a residential answer and present it as the underwriting.

- Mixed-use is a `rental` or `condo` program with `commercial` rows in the same program: the pro forma prices the homes and the commercial income together, capitalizing the commercial NOI beside a condo sellout. Draw the commercial area from the ground-floor or commercial-overlay capacity, not from floor area the homes need.
- `commercial` takes `units: []` and commercial rows only, valued at the commercial cap rate you state in `capRate`, with debt coverage and cash on cash like a rental. Community facility is the `community_facility` use; size it from the community-facility FAR band, and say plainly that no community-facility envelope or bulk scenario is modeled yet, so the area is a stated program, not a massing result.
- Commercial rents by use go in `acres_market_inputs` as `commercialRents`, with the same source discipline as residential rents: retail, office and community-facility rents come from retrieved listings, broker reports or the user, never from a residential figure.
- In `acres_compare_programs`, a commercial variant compares on the same land, cost and financing inputs as the residential ones, and `rentScale` on it moves commercial rents. Compare a commercial or mixed-use variant beside the residential ones whenever the site, the zoning or the ask makes it a real contender, and say which question each variant answers.

The combined apartment net area (each count times its unit size) and commercial net area must fit inside total gross building area. This also applies to comparison size overrides and full program replacements. Correct the building area or program when it does not fit; do not silently resize apartments. An edited saved unit mix can remain saved while an older financial model stays stale because its stated building area is too small. Reverting to a fitting mix revalidates unchanged figures without creating a different model snapshot.

## Ledger-sourced inputs (NYC)

Where the NYC development ledger covers the subject, a measured public record beats a figure quoted secondhand, so prefer the ledger sources and keep stated figures for what the ledger cannot measure:

- Land: `landPriceSource: 'ledger'` on `acres_pro_forma` prices land at what nearby development sites sold for, each earlier sale in the lot's borough within a mile (widened to 1.5, then 2 miles where a mile holds fewer than five; the basis states the distance) adjusted to the lot, per as-of-right buildable SF times the lot's as-of-right area: the figure the site page shows, backtested on held-out sold sites. Only where that model gives no figure (fewer than five such sales, no coordinates, or no as-of-right residential area; the basis says which) does it fall back to the median price per max ZFA of the block's arm's-length ACRIS deeds, which the same held-out test found far less accurate; `inputs.landPriceBasis.method` says which (`nearby_model` or `block_median`), and the answer should say so too. Prefer it when the user has no contract or asking price; a stated price for this parcel outranks comps. It fails with a reason (no nearby model and fewer than three priced deeds, district not curated, ledger not synced); then ask for or retrieve a land price instead of loosening the method.
- Pipeline: `pipelineSource: 'ledger'` on `acres_market_inputs` records DOB filings in the subject ZIP. Prefer it over a press-reported unit count; ZIP filings are neither a submarket boundary nor a delivery schedule, so the horizon is the filing window.
- Taxes: `taxSource: 'ledger'` (rentals and commercial) adds a separate tax line from the DOF assessed value times the class rate, and `residentialOpexPerUnitPerYear` must then exclude taxes. It describes the parcel as assessed today, before exemptions and phase-ins; a new building is reassessed, so for new construction treat it as a floor and say so, or keep a stated tax-inclusive opex when the user has a tax projection or an abatement path.

## NYC new-rental tax incentive (485-x)

A new NYC rental of six or more units carries full class 2 taxes unless it takes 485-x, and those taxes are one of the largest lines in the model, so a new-construction rental underwritten without a tax path understates or overstates value. Run `taxIncentive` as an explicit scenario beside the full-tax case rather than assuming the exemption. The program class turns on unit count and zone: under 100 units (Option B) there is no construction wage floor, at 100 Option A raises the affordable share and adds the wage floor, and at 150 in Zone A or B the project becomes very large, deeper affordability (60% AMI) and a much higher floor in exchange for 40 years. Where the envelope allows, compare the program just below and at each line (99 and 100, 149 and 150 units) before recommending a count; an Option A pro forma reports the same building held to 99 units (`at99Units`), and the deal screen's `rentalSchemes` list the full-tax, 485-x and 99-unit buildings it priced, at screening precision. Report the option, restricted units, benefit years, wage floor and whether the floor figure is an upper bound, the present value of the taxes exempted and the result without 485-x. A project labor agreement or a waiving collective bargaining agreement removes the wage floor; building service workers at 30+ units earn prevailing wages, an operating cost to reflect in opex. Zone, eligibility and rents are statutory or HPD facts; the DOF cap rate, labor share and expected wages are assumptions to state.

When you use a ledger source, report its basis line from the derivation: the sample size and range of price per ZFA with the ACRIS document ids, the DOB job count and filing window, or the assessed value, class rate and both fiscal years with the market-value cross-check, plus the ledger retrieval date and the official-record citation.

`acres_compare_programs` compares rental, condo, commercial, mixed-use and affordable variants on one set of inputs, ranked by profit after incremental approval land carry, with ordinal entitlement scores reported separately and never treated as approval probabilities or multipliers on dollars. Missing timing remains unpriced and the ranking provisional, and returns the recommended plan plus the conditions that would flip the ranking. Use it when the user asks which program to pursue, after market inputs, unit mix and (where relevant) entitlement scenarios.

The same tool records the acquisition decision, which is what the site page shows under "Should you buy it". Whenever an answer prices a deal or reaches a buy, price or pass verdict, run it on the pro forma's inputs, with one variant when only one program is viable, and give `targetReturnOnCost` from the user or as a labeled planning hurdle. State the verdict, the highest land price at that return and the rent or price change the stated land price needs from its `decision`, never from hand arithmetic on the pro forma.

That decision prices the lot for building new. When the buyer means to keep the building (hold it, renovate and rent it, or renovate and sell it), price it with `acres_keep_building` and load the `keep-building` skill; a full underwriting runs both, and the answer should say which question each verdict answers.

Comparison variant numbers are checked before ledger retrieval or capture. Affordable unit share runs from 0 to 1; the restricted/contract rent ratio must be nonnegative. Entitlement scores run from 0 to 100, approval timelines from 0 to 60 months, and building-size overrides from 500 to 20,000,000 gross SF. Rent multipliers must be greater than 0 and at most 2; condo price multipliers must be positive. Use numbers rather than numeric strings. Omitted or null optional values keep their defaults; invalid supplied values must be corrected rather than clamped or silently ignored. The blended rent multiplier must remain greater than 0 and at most 2, including when a positive contract rent ratio exceeds 1.

`acres_search_deal_store` screens the internal NYC deal store (HomeHarvest listings plus listings NYC investment-sales brokers publish on their own sites, both matched to PLUTO) for listed parcels with unused buildable floor area. It is internal-only: the store is readable by admin accounts, or by everyone once it is enabled, and only admins can make it sync an empty borough or ZIP (`syncIfEmpty`). Filters and sort options are in the tool schema. Each deal's `flashcardValuation` carries the sliding scale in $/buildable sq ft and a `basis` list giving the date and origin of every cost, cap rate, submarket benchmark and ZFA-to-gross deduction behind it. Those are dated planning assumptions, and ZIP medians are an asking price sample from an unlicensed listing source: report them as screening estimates with that basis, never as market evidence, and confirm any figure a decision depends on through `acres_market_inputs` with a real source. `listingSource` names where a deal came from. A broker-site listing is that broker's own published offering with its asking price when one is posted; many are price on request and stay unpriced, and the ZIP benchmarks never include broker rows.

Every figure in a feasibility answer must be traceable: retrieved source, user assumption, or labeled planning baseline. Never present a baseline or a sensitivity as a market fact.
